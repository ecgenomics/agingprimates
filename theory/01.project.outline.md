# Project outline

## Working title

Comparative genomics of longevity across primates: an update of Farré et al.
(2021) using the Kuderna et al. (2023) phylogeny and genomic resource.

## General question

Which genes and amino-acid substitutions show convergent evolutionary patterns
associated with unusually long or short maximum lifespan in primates after
accounting for the allometric relationship between lifespan and body mass?

## Relationship to the previous study

Farré et al. (2021) studied longevity across mammals using a longevity quotient
(LQ), convergent amino-acid substitutions (CAAS), and associations between
phenotypes and gene-specific evolutionary rates. The present project adapts
that logic to the denser primate sampling made possible by Kuderna et al.
(2023). It is not intended to reuse previous outputs blindly: inputs and each
derived result will be reconstructed, checked, and documented in order.

## Core workflow

1. **Assemble independent inputs.** Collect the selected phylogenetic tree,
   taxonomic reconciliation, maximum lifespan, adult body mass, genome/sample
   availability, and later the genomic alignments or gene trees required by
   CAAS and RERconverge.
2. **Reconcile taxonomy.** Map trait names and genomic sample names to the 236
   tip labels of the selected Kuderna S4 tree. Every rename, exclusion,
   duplicate, and unresolved taxon must be recorded.
3. **Curate the two source traits.** Establish one justified maximum-lifespan
   value and one adult-body-mass value per species, retaining original units,
   source, and any transformation.
4. **Construct the allometrically corrected longevity trait.** Reproduce the
   historical longevity quotient and compare it with a primate-specific
   phylogenetic allometric model as a sensitivity analysis.
5. **Quantify phylogenetic signal.** Estimate signal for maximum lifespan, body
   mass, and the corrected longevity trait on the Kuderna S4 time tree.
6. **Define genomic contrasts.** Decide whether to use continuous LQ, extreme
   quantiles, within-family top/bottom pairs, or more than one preregistered
   contrast. Thresholds must be fixed before examining gene-level results.
7. **Run CAAS analyses.** Identify substitutions shared by independently
   long-lived or short-lived lineages, with explicit discovery, validation,
   background, missing-data, and multiple-testing rules.
8. **Run RERconverge analyses.** Associate relative evolutionary rates with the
   continuous and/or binary longevity phenotype while respecting tree and
   taxon coverage.
9. **Integrate evidence.** Compare CAAS genes, rate-associated genes, the Farré
   et al. 2021 gene set, known aging genes, and functional/pathway enrichment.
10. **Sensitivity analyses.** Repeat key steps under alternative trait sources,
    allometric corrections, taxonomic decisions, thresholds, and suitable
    alternative trees or pruned taxon sets.

## Current input state

The operational tree is `inputs/science.abn7829_data_s4.nex.tree`, an independent, byte-identical
copy of Supplementary Data S4 from Kuderna et al. (2023). It contains 236 tips
and is rooted, fully bifurcating, fossil-calibrated, and ultrametric. All tips
have a root-to-tip distance of approximately 69.06116 time units (approximately
millions of years). Its checksum and provenance are recorded alongside it.

The remaining files presently under `inputs/` are historical candidates. Their
presence does not yet designate them as approved inputs for the reconstructed
analysis.

## Planned primary outputs

- a taxon reconciliation and exclusion audit;
- a provenance-rich species trait table;
- a reproducible allometric-model report and final longevity phenotype;
- phylogenetic-signal estimates and diagnostic plots;
- frozen continuous and binary phenotype/configuration files;
- CAAS and RERconverge result tables with multiple-testing correction;
- integrated gene lists and enrichment results;
- a complete decision log and reproducible analysis record.

## Principles and decisions to preserve

- Raw inputs, curated inputs, intermediate data, and results remain separate.
- Species are never silently renamed, imputed, averaged, or removed.
- Units are explicit in column names and metadata.
- The allometric equation, fitting population, and tree are part of the trait
  definition, not incidental implementation details.
- Genome availability is applied only after the phenotypic dataset has been
  audited, so missing phenotypes are not confused with missing genomes.
- Exploratory thresholds are not presented as prespecified confirmatory tests.
- Nominal gene or site-level P values are not treated as final significance
  without an explicit multiple-testing strategy.

## Immediate next steps

1. Audit candidate maximum-lifespan and body-mass columns and their sources.
2. Reconcile their species labels to the Kuderna S4 tree.
3. Reproduce the historical fixed-formula LQ exactly.
4. Fit and diagnose a primate-specific phylogenetic allometric model.
5. Compare the two corrected traits and then estimate phylogenetic signal.
